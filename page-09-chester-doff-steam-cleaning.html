DOFF Steam Cleaning in Chester: Superheated Steam for Stone and Brick

Some cleaning jobs need heat more than pressure. DOFF steam cleaning is a method that uses superheated steam — water heated well above boiling point — delivered at low pressure to clean stone, brick, render and other masonry. It was developed for heritage and conservation work, where the surface is too valuable or too fragile for abrasive or high-pressure methods, and it has become the standard approach for cleaning historic buildings across the UK.

The principle is simple. Steam at around 150 degrees Celsius softens and lifts dirt, paint, biological growth and pollution deposits without the mechanical force of a pressure washer. Because the pressure is low, the surface itself is not eroded. The high temperature also kills algae, moss and lichen at the root, which means growth takes much longer to return than after a cold-water wash. And because the system uses very little water, there is minimal run-off and the risk of saturating the masonry is low.

Why Chester Needs DOFF Cleaning

Few British cities suit this method better than Chester. The city's sandstone buildings — from the Rows to the city walls, churches, schools and countless Victorian terraces — are built from a soft, porous stone that high-pressure washing can damage in a single pass. Lime mortar, which is softer than modern cement mortar, is also easily stripped by a jet washer. DOFF steam cleaning is the approach conservation officers and heritage bodies recommend for these materials, and it is widely specified for listed buildings and buildings in conservation areas.

What DOFF Cleaning Removes

The method is effective on a wide range of soiling: carbon and traffic pollution that has blackened stone over decades, algae and lichen, bird fouling, old paint and graffiti, and the general grime that accumulates on any urban building. Paint removal is a particular strength — steam softens paint so it can be lifted without the chemicals or abrasives that would damage the surface underneath. Each job is different, though, and a responsible operator will always test a small, inconspicuous area first to confirm the result before committing to the whole facade.

The Process and What to Expect

A DOFF clean begins with a survey of the building: its materials, condition, any previous treatments, and the type of soiling. Scaffolding or access equipment is arranged where needed. The operator works in sections, holding the steam lance at a consistent distance and moving steadily so the heat does the work. Progress is checked constantly, because the aim is to remove the dirt and stop there — not to over-clean and leave the stone looking unnaturally bright. A well-judged DOFF clean leaves a building looking cared for rather than newly built, which is exactly right for a historic facade.
