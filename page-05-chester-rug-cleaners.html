Rug Cleaners in Chester: Caring for Wool, Silk and Handmade Rugs

A good rug is both a furnishing and an investment. In Chester homes they range from practical machine-made hall runners to hand-knotted wool rugs and delicate silk pieces that have been in a family for decades. Each type needs different care, and the wrong cleaning method can cause damage that cannot be undone: colours can bleed, wool can felt and shrink, fringes can tangle and break, and a silk rug can be ruined by too much water. This is why rugs deserve specialist attention rather than being treated like wall-to-wall carpet.

The first step in any proper rug clean is identification. A professional will check the fibre — wool, cotton, silk, viscose or synthetic — the construction, the dyes and the condition of the backing and fringes. A dye test is essential on handmade rugs: a damp white cloth pressed to the colours shows whether any dye is likely to run. Only then is the cleaning method chosen. Handmade and natural-fibre rugs are usually cleaned off-site, where they can be dusted, washed and dried under controlled conditions.

What a Professional Rug Clean Involves

A thorough rug clean starts with dry soil removal. Rugs hold a remarkable amount of dry grit deep in the pile, and this grit acts like sandpaper on the fibres every time the rug is walked on. Professional dusting — beating or vibrating the rug from the back — removes far more than any vacuum can. The rug is then washed with a solution suited to its fibres, rinsed, and dried flat with good airflow so it does not distort. Fringes are cleaned and combed separately. The whole process takes longer than carpet cleaning, but it restores colour and texture in a way that surface cleaning cannot.

Looking After Rugs Between Cleans

Day-to-day care makes a large difference. Vacuum rugs regularly on a low suction setting, and turn the beater bar off for looped or handmade piles. Rotate the rug every few months so wear and sunlight are spread evenly. Deal with spills at once by blotting, never rubbing, and never use a supermarket carpet shampoo on a handmade rug — the chemicals can strip natural oils from wool and leave residues that attract dirt. If a rug has been in a damp room, check the backing: a musty smell is a warning that moisture is trapped underneath.

When to Have a Rug Professionally Cleaned

As a rule, a rug in a busy room benefits from professional cleaning every one to two years, and sooner after a spill, a pet accident, or a period in storage. It is also worth having a rug cleaned before it is stored away or put on display after a move. A cleaner should be able to explain the method they will use for your particular rug and give a realistic drying time.
